# On Cloud Shoes: Which Cloud Model Fits You, Where to Buy, and How to Stack Cashback on Top

People searching for On Cloud shoes usually aren't confused about the brand. They know they want the Swiss shoes with the holes in the sole. The confusion starts one click later, when the lineup turns out to be a dozen different shoes that all begin with the word Cloud, all cost somewhere between mid-$100s and $400, and all look reasonably similar in a product photo.

That's the real question behind this search: which one, at what price, from whom — and is there any way to not pay the sticker price twice over. This is a walk through the current On Cloud range, what the models actually differ on, where prices move, and how a cashback layer affects the final number.

## "On Cloud shoes" is at least four different shoes

The Cloud name is doing a lot of work at On. The main families you'll run into:

- **Cloud / Cloud 5 / Cloud 6** — the original everyday shoe. Light, quick to put on, designed as much for commuting and standing around as for running.
- **Cloudmonster** — On's max-cushion daily trainer. Bigger CloudTec pods, more foam, noticeably heavier.
- **Cloudswift / Cloudsurfer / Cloudrunner** — road running, split by ride feel: city-run cushioning, smooth rolling, and stability respectively.
- **Cloudtilt / Cloudnova / Cloud X** — lifestyle and cross-training. The Cloudtilt is the one that ended up in fashion collabs and now sells for lifestyle prices.
- **Cloudhorizon / Cloudsoma** — trail.

A large share of "On Cloud shoes" searches are really people trying to work out whether they want the Cloud 6 or the Cloudmonster. Those two are the volume sellers, and they're not interchangeable.

Specs matter more than colourways here, because the differences show up on your feet after about an hour.

The practical read: Cloud 6 is the shoe for people who want one pair and want it comfortable straight out of the box. The revised opening and sockliner exist specifically to fix the "why is this so hard to get on" complaint from earlier Clouds. The 8 mm drop pushes you slightly forward, which is fine on flat pavement and less fine if you prefer a totally flat ride.

Cloudmonster is a different proposition. On's own product description calls it "our weirdest creation" and pairs the oversized pods with a Speedboard for energy return on take-off. It is the most cushioned thing in the everyday range, and at roughly 300 g it is also the heaviest. If you're buying On shoes mainly to walk around a city, the Cloudmonster is more shoe than the job requires.

## The sizing problem that shows up after delivery

On shoes have a fit reputation worth planning for. Nordstrom's listing for the Cloud 6 Waterproof states plainly that customers report the fit runs slightly small, and its fit guidance recommends ordering half a size up. That's one retailer's data, not a universal law, but it points at something real: the heel geometry and step-in opening changed with this generation, and reviewers have complained about pressure on the ankle bone on certain foot shapes.

The implication is simply that On Cloud shoes are a bad candidate for blind buying. If you can't try a pair in a shop, buy from a seller with a free or cheap return policy, order your usual size and the half size up if you're between sizes, and return one pair. The return policy matters more than a 10% discount here.

## Where to buy: official store, sports retailers, or marketplaces

Three routes, each with a different trade-off.

**On's own store** gives you the full size run, the newest colourways, and the guaranteed-authentic pair. Discounts are rare outside end-of-season.

**Sports retailers** are where the price swings. JD Sports in Singapore carries the widest current-season On range locally, and multi-brand European retailers like Galaxus regularly run 10–25% off individual models. Japanese department stores sit in the same bracket as official retail pricing.

**Marketplaces** like Lazada, Shopee and Amazon Singapore carry both official brand stores and third-party sellers. Prices can be lower, but parallel imports and delivery timelines are a real consideration — check whether the listing is from the brand's official store before you commit.

One more thing worth knowing: On runs collaborations and limited drops at much higher prices. Cloudtilt collab colourways have listed around US$200, and a LightSpray Cloudmonster collaboration hit US$990. Those are collector prices for different colourways of the same shoe, not better shoes.
